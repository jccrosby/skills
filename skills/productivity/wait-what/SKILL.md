# SKILL: wait-what

## When to Use

- A previous answer, proposal, plan, or recommendation needs a clearer pitch.
- The idea is sound, but the wording is too long, too vague, or too generic.
- You need to explain the idea in a way that fits the current project, not in a broad abstract frame.
- The message must be simple, direct, and easy to understand for a technical or business audience.

## Procedure

1. Read the previous response or proposal.
2. Identify the core message: what is being proposed, why it matters, and what decision or action is needed.
3. Check the current project context. Use the active workspace, repo, task, or project goal as the source of truth.
4. Reframe the idea from the current project perspective. State how it fits the project, its priority, its outcome, and its constraints.
5. Rewrite the content in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.
6. Keep each sentence short and direct. Use active voice. Avoid idioms, hype, and vague business language.
7. Make the response practical: what the project gains, what is required, what risk exists, and what the next step is.
8. End with a clear recommendation or action.

## ASD-STE100 Rules

- Use short sentences.
- Use one idea per sentence.
- Use plain words.
- Do not use sarcasm, jokes, or idioms.
- Do not use marketing language.
- Define terms if they may be unclear.
- Prefer direct language over abstract language.
- Keep the focus on the project, the user, and the result.

## Output Format

### Re-pitch

- Current project context:
- What we are doing:
- Why this matters now:
- What the project gains:
- What we need to do:
- Risk or constraint:
- Recommended next step:

Then provide a short, polished summary in plain ASD-STE100 wording.

## Quality Checks

- The final message is grounded in the current project.
- The message is shorter and clearer than the original response.
- The wording is plain and easy to read.
- The message is useful to a decision maker or project team.
- The output ends with a practical next step.

## Example Prompt

"Use the wait-what skill to re-pitch my prior recommendation in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, from the perspective of this project. Keep it short, direct, and action-focused."

## Constraint

Do not invent facts. Use the prior answer and the current project context. If a fact is missing, state the gap clearly and keep the message honest.
